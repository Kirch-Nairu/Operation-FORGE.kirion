# Product Engineering System

Product engineering is the discipline of connecting engineering decisions to the problem a product exists to solve, so that technical correctness and product value are evaluated together rather than as separate, sometimes conflicting, concerns handled by different people at different times.

The failure this exists to prevent is specific: a feature can be engineered correctly — well-tested, performant, well-architected — and still fail, because it solved a problem nobody had, or solved the right problem in a way that doesn't fit how people actually use the product. Engineering quality and product-market fit are independent axes, and excelling on one says nothing about the other.

The practical discipline is keeping the connection between a change and the problem it addresses explicit and checkable, not assumed. Before building, what is the specific user or business problem this solves, and how will it be known whether it solved it? After shipping, was that question actually answered, or did the team move to the next feature before the evidence came in either way?

This is where product engineering and delivery lanes meet decision-evidence lanes: a shipped feature without a defined success signal is a bet with no way to learn whether it paid off, which means the next similar bet is made with the same amount of information as this one — none gained from having actually shipped it.
